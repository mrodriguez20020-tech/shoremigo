# Aurora Tide visual foundation

`island-ocean.webp` is an original AI-generated fictional island/ocean plate. `aurora-tide.webp` is an original AI-generated fictional boutique exploration cruiser with transparent background, rendered against that plate's light and camera reference. Both were created for ShoreMigo with OpenAI image generation on 2026-11-11, then encoded locally as WebP. No real cruise-line ship photograph, logo or third-party character is used.

The vessel keeps pearl-white tiered observation decks, a deep navy hull, restrained sea-glass trim, panoramic dark glazing and contemporary architectural curves. The same transparent asset is reused in intro, welcome, hero, golden hour and the Ships & Rooms discovery card. Color grading, subtle scene transforms and the coded wake are presentation layers, not live 3D or a simulated ocean.

These are non-code concept assets, excluded from the repository's MIT source-code license. The task's generated output is available to the user under applicable OpenAI terms, including commercial use. Authorship/copyright protection for AI-generated imagery may vary by jurisdiction. See ASSETS.md for the repository's asset policy.

For future Ships & Rooms work, reuse this exact silhouette, glazing bands, lifeboat placement, hull colors and deck tiers. A single 2D concept view is not a dimensional vessel model or a verified deck plan. The existing ship explorer and cabin diagrams remain fictional functional demos; matching new angles or spatial cabin geometry requires an authored vessel model/design sheet. No such model is claimed here.

Generation direction: realistic elevated port-aft three-quarter view, bow toward upper right; a restrained modern luxury exploration cruiser of roughly 160 m with five to six observation tiers; no branding or theme-park amenities. Ocean plate: calm Caribbean-inspired sea, distant lush mountain island to the right, natural afternoon light from upper right, open editorial space left, no ships or buildings. The vessel was generated separately using the ocean plate only as a light/camera reference. Migo artwork was not used or regenerated.
